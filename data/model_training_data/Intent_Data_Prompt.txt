You are helping me create a synthetic training dataset for an intent classifier used in a grounded natural-language shoe-world application.


Generate approximately 200 unique English commands for each of the nine supported intent classes listed below. Complex actions should have more commands than simple actions.
Each command must have exactly one primary intent. The language should resemble commands that a user could give to an assistant operating in the described shoe world.
The dataset will later be inspected and corrected manually, so prioritize linguistic diversity while keeping the intended label identifiable.

Supported intents
#################

Use exactly these intent labels:
- PICK_UP: pick up or take a shoe or tool into the agent's hand
- PUT_DOWN: put down an object currently being held, optionally at a specified location or next to another object
- MOVE: move or relocate an object from its current position to another location
- CLEAN: clean a shoe, optionally using a cleaning utensil
- IMPREGNATE: waterproof, protect, wax, cream, or impregnate a shoe
- DRY: dry a wet or damp shoe
- REPAIR: repair the sole or material of a shoe using an appropriate repair tool
- GET_NEW_TOOL: fetch or obtain a fresh or replacement tool
- GO_ON_WALK: go on a walk or run while wearing a specified shoe
Do not create additional labels

World description
#################
The world contains shoes, tools, storage locations, and walk settings.

Shoes
-----
A shoe may be described using any combination of:
- Type: sandal, sneaker, boot, or the generic term shoe
- Color: red, green, blue, yellow, black, white, brown
- Material: leather, suede, rubber, canvas
- Height: low, mid, high
- Cleaning state: clean, a little dirty, medium dirty, dirty, very dirty
- Material state: good, scratched, cracked
- Sole state: intact, worn, loose, damaged
- Protection state: protected, partly protected, unprotected
- Dry state: dry, wet
- Dirt type: mud, dust, sand, grass, oil

Natural aliases and variations may also be used, for example:
- sneakers, trainers, running shoes
- boots, sandals
- tan or beige for brown
- navy or dark blue for blue
- fabric or cloth for canvas
- tall for high and small for low
- spotless, slightly dirty, filthy
- waterproof, partially protected, not waterproof
- damp or soaked
- muddy, dusty, sandy, grassy, oily

Tools
-----
Cleaning tools:
- cleaner
- brush
- rag
Impregnation tools:
- spray
- cream
Repair tools:
- sole glue
- sole hammer
- needle and yarn

Use natural aliases where appropriate, such as cleaning cloth, towel, shoe cleaner, waterproofing spray, wax, glue, hammer, needle and thread.

Locations
---------

Objects may be located:
- on the top/middle/bottom shelf
- in or on the floor box
- in the drying area
- in the tool area
- in the agent's hand
Natural variants such as floor, ground, drying spot, drying corner, tool box, tool zone, rack, and in hand are allowed.

Walk properties
---------------
Walk locations may include:
- park
- beach
- forest
- street
- city
- playground
- meadow
- field
- garden
- garage
- workshop
- parking lot
- construction site
- trail
Walk lengths may be short, medium, or long, including aliases such as brief, quick, regular, normal, extended, or far.
Weather may be sunny or rainy, including phrases such as in the sun, in dry weather, in the rain, in wet weather, or during stormy weather.

Object references
-----------------
Commands should use a mixture of reference styles.

Attribute descriptions:
Examples:
- the red leather boot
- the wet canvas sneaker
- the dirty low sandal
- the shoe with the damaged sole

Object IDs:
Some commands should refer to explicit object IDs. Plausible ID forms include:
- boot_2
- sneaker_12
- shoe_6
-...
Do not use IDs in every sentence. Mix ID-based references with ordinary descriptions.

Spatial and nested references:
Include commands that identify objects through their relationships to other objects, for example:
- the sandal next to the blue boot
- the brush beside the cleaner in the tool area
- the sneaker next to the red boot on the middle shelf
- the shoe on the floor next to rag_4
- the canvas boot beside the sandal in the drying area
Some examples should contain nested descriptions. They must remain understandable even when more than one spatial phrase occurs.
For MOVE and PUT_DOWN commands, earlier relations can identify the object, while the final spatial phrase can describe its destination.
Example:
move the green sandal next to the boot onto the top shelf
Here, "next to the boot" identifies the sandal, and "onto the top shelf" is the movement target.

Numerical tool constraints
--------------------------
Include around special examples in which a tool is selected by a numerical state.
Cleaning and impregnation utensils have a remaining fullness percentage. Examples:
- a cleaner that is at least 30% full
- the brush with more than 40 percent left
- a spray with minimum fullness of 60%
- cream that is not below 50% full

Repair tools have a damage percentage. Examples:
- sole glue that is at most 25% damaged
- a hammer with less than 30 percent damage
- needle and yarn that is no more than 15% damaged
- a repair tool whose damage does not exceed 40%

Use percentages between 10% and 90%. Vary the expression of the comparison:

- at least
- minimum
- more than
- above
- over
- not below
- at most
- maximum
- less than

These numerical constraints should mainly occur in CLEAN, IMPREGNATE, and REPAIR examples.

Linguistic diversity
--------------------
Use a broad range of formulations:
- direct imperatives
- polite requests
- questions
- short commands
- longer descriptive commands
- commands with object IDs
- commands with “next to” or “beside” relations
- commands with nested references
- commands with omitted optional information
- natural synonyms for the main action

Some difficult examples may contain words associated with another action as background context, but the primary request must remain clear. For example:
- clean the boot before putting it away -> CLEAN
- get a new brush before cleaning the shoe -> GET_NEW_TOOL

Do not generate commands that genuinely request two separate actions.

Distribution requirements
-------------------------

For each intent, include a mixture of:
- around 30% simple commands
- around 25% commands containing multiple shoe attributes
- around 20% commands containing object IDs
- around 15% commands containing locations or next-to references
- around 10% challenging commands containing nested references, conditions, or numerical constraints where appropriate
Avoid excessive repetition of sentence templates. Do not merely replace colors or object names in otherwise identical sentences.


Output format
-------------

Return only semicolon-separated CSV text with "command;intent".
Each following line must contain one command and one uppercase intent label:
pick up the red boot;PICK_UP
clean the dirty sneaker with the brush;CLEAN

Do not wrap the output in Markdown and do not include any text before or after the CSV.
